Property Transformation Website Requirements

Business and software specification

Prepared for Rudyard Madoro and the business owner | Updated 4 October 2026 | Version 1.2

# 1 Purpose and recommended direction

Build a professional, mobile friendly website that turns the owner's experience in transforming older houses into rental and short stay units into a credible service offering for other property owners. The website must demonstrate completed work, explain the delivery process and generate actionable enquiries for construction, renovations, conversions, extensions, interior design, cabinetry, maintenance and supporting materials services.

The recommended launch is a portfolio and lead management platform with a practical administration area. Its service structure must give property transformation, custom cabinetry and board sales clear commercial identities. Board sales are a strong standalone business serving homeowners, carpenters, installers, contractors and DIY customers, whether or not they purchase construction services. Customers can buy boards, request cutting and related workshop services, or commission supply-only and complete fix-and-supply cabinetry and woodwork. Selected hardware remains a supporting offer. Each customer should immediately understand the available purchase or service options and the next step after enquiring.

This specification is intended for implementation planning, UX design, backlog creation, development and acceptance testing. All requirements labelled Must are launch requirements; Should requirements are the next improvements; Could requirements need separate prioritisation. Estimated effort and suggested commercial policies are planning recommendations, not supplier quotations or existing business commitments.

# 2 Business facts assumptions and decisions

## 2 1 Established business context

The owner has three residential properties. He buys older houses with additional land, converts existing buildings into bachelor and one bedroom units, and builds additional adjoining units. Units are used for rentals and some for Airbnb. He develops his own layouts and interior and exterior style. A team already performs construction, renovations, conversions, extensions, maintenance and new construction. A cabinetry workshop has machinery and equipment producing kitchens, wardrobes, vanities, TV units and furniture components.

The commercial offering includes services for other property owners, standalone board sales, and cabinetry and related woodwork. Board supply is an established part of the offering and must be prominently marketed in its own right. Boards may be sold for customer projects or used by the business to manufacture and install kitchens, cabinets, wardrobes, vanities, TV units and other confirmed woodwork. General hardware retail, a paid assessment service, professional design partnerships, project financing and property management remain proposals requiring owner confirmation.

## 2 2 Planning assumptions

Assume an English language launch and a primarily local service market. South Africa is the provisional jurisdiction for privacy and professional practice considerations; confirm the country, municipality, service radius and currency before finalising policies or publishing local claims. If the business operates elsewhere, adapt these requirements to that jurisdiction. The business name, domain, logo, legal entity, addresses and contact details remain owner decisions. Do not assume that the project context name Takura Properties is the approved trading name.

Assume the website markets services rather than purchasing properties from the public or offering investment participation. The owner must clarify whether clients pay for work under ordinary service contracts, enter joint ventures, lease their land or receive a managed development service. Launch should present a client funded service unless a different commercial model is expressly approved.

Assume the existing properties can be photographed with appropriate permissions. No unit counts, project costs, completion dates, qualifications, certifications, rental yields, testimonials or guarantees may be invented. Existing owner projects should be labelled as the company's own portfolio developments when applicable.

## 2 3 Pending business decisions and placeholder policy

Development and design may proceed using labelled, configurable dummy values. The following business decisions will be updated as they are confirmed; actual claims and fulfilment policies require approval before live publication. Confirm the trading name and service territory; immediately saleable services; board ranges, brands, finishes, thicknesses, full-sheet sizes and stock or sourcing arrangements; minimum order quantities; workshop capabilities and limits; cutting, edging and drilling availability; cabinetry installation scope; and collection and delivery rules. Board selling is confirmed as a standalone business. The detailed product range and fulfilment policies must still be agreed before publication. Confirm how external customers will be served without disrupting existing property work.

Agree who owns incoming enquiries, who prepares and approves quotes, whether site visits are free or paid, the business hours and response target, quote validity, warranty terms and customer contract process. Identify any registered design or engineering partners and evidence supporting registration or accreditation claims. Agree photography permissions, available content, implementation budget, operating budget and maintenance responsibility.

# 3 Business positioning and service structure

## 3 1 Main value proposition

Proposed message: We help property owners transform older homes and underused space into practical, well finished accommodation, with construction, interiors and cabinetry coordinated through one delivery team.

Present operational experience, board supply and control of the cabinetry workshop as concrete strengths. Give standalone board customers a clear path to product information and quotations. For property and cabinetry customers, show how coordinated board selection, fabrication, installation, interiors and construction simplify delivery. Treat additional rental income as a possible customer objective, never a guaranteed outcome. Feasibility, approvals, market demand, costs and operating conditions determine the result.

## 3 3 Possible commercial packages

Offer clearly defined commercial options: boards only; boards with cutting and confirmed edging or drilling; supply-only manufactured cabinetry or furniture components; and complete fix-and-supply cabinetry and woodwork. Fix and supply means the business supplies the agreed materials, manufactures or prepares the work, and installs it at the customer site under an approved scope. Apply these options to kitchens, cabinets, wardrobes, vanities, TV units and other confirmed woodwork. Also offer property assessment, conversion and construction, rental-ready fit out and short-stay styling where operationally validated. Each option needs deliverables, exclusions, pricing basis, delivery or installation terms and responsibility for measurement verification. Plumbing, electrical work, structural changes, appliances and countertops are included only when expressly quoted.

# 4 Audiences goals and measures

## 4 1 Customer groups

Property owners need evidence that an older house or spare land can be assessed responsibly, and want clarity on scope, cost and disruption. Landlords and small developers care about durable finishes, repeatable unit layouts, maintenance and staged delivery. Short stay operators care about presentation, storage, cleaning, furnishing and photography readiness. Homeowners buying cabinetry need material choices, dimensions, installation scope and examples. Standalone board buyers include carpenters, cabinet makers, installers, contractors, homeowners and DIY customers. They need product specifications, full-sheet or cut-to-size options, quantities, price quotations and reliable collection or delivery information. Board buyers must be served without being required to purchase cabinetry or construction services.

Tenants and Airbnb guests are a secondary audience. The main website should not route construction enquiries into accommodation booking journeys. If accommodation is later advertised, provide a clearly separate section linking to verified booking listings.

# 5 Scope and release priorities

Explain design discussion, measurement verification, board and hardware selection, manufacture, installation where offered and handover. Clearly present supply-only and fix-and-supply options. Fix-and-supply quotes cover the agreed board materials, fabrication, fittings and installation labour, with explicit exclusions. Show finish and storage examples with captions, workshop information and links to the standalone board sales range.

Clearly offer boards for customers who fabricate or install their own cabinetry, wardrobes and related woodwork, and boards used in the business's supply-only or fix-and-supply services. # 7 Customer journeys and interaction design

## 7 1 Property owner journey

A visitor arrives from search or referral, reviews conversion examples, reads the assessment process, selects their project type, provides suburb, property description, intended use and available photos, and requests contact. Staff qualify the location and ownership or authorisation, clarify the objective, arrange an assessment if appropriate, prepare a scoped quote, follow up and record the outcome.

## 7 2 Cabinetry journey

A visitor reviews examples, chooses kitchen, cabinets, wardrobes or another product, selects supply only or fix and supply, provides approximate dimensions or requests measuring help, selects a finish preference if known and submits photos or a sketch. The cabinetry team confirms measurements, site conditions, board materials, fittings, fabrication and any installation scope before issuing a formal quote. The customer may instead follow a link to buy boards for their own fabrication work.

## 7 3 Workshop customer journey

A trade or DIY customer opens Board Sales, selects a confirmed board product or describes a requirement, specifies full sheets or cut-to-size supply, adds quantity and collection or delivery preference, and requests a quote. No cutting list is required for full-sheet sales. Customers requesting cutting upload a list or request assistance and specify any edging or drilling. Staff confirm product availability, processing capability, price and lead time. Workshop production begins only after the approved specifications and commercial conditions are confirmed.

## 7 4 General experience rules

FR 10 Must. Keep forms short initially, reveal only relevant fields and explain why important information is requested. Provide Back and Next, a clear progress indicator, review before submission and field level error messages that preserve entered data. Allow Not sure for budget, dimensions, intended unit count and materials when reasonable. Do not require a precise street address at first contact.

Use a mobile bottom action bar only if it does not obscure content, form controls or accessibility focus. Keep WhatsApp as an alternative contact path, not the sole route. Opening a WhatsApp link must be user initiated and contain no private form data by default.

Acceptance: A customer can complete a valid basic enquiry without attachments, a budget figure or an account. A maintenance enquiry can be completed through a short version. Validation errors place focus on an error summary and preserve entered values.

## 8 3 Cutting list specification

FR 13 Must. At launch accept a PDF, image or XLSX cutting list and provide clear downloadable or on page instructions. List fields are part identifier, material or board code, finish or colour, thickness in millimetres, length, width, quantity, grain direction, four individual edge treatments, drilling instructions and notes. State whether submitted measurements mean finished part sizes or saw cut sizes; the workshop must select and document one convention before launch.

Define edge references with a labelled diagram or instructions: length side A, length side B, width side A and width side B. Record edging material and thickness rather than a single ambiguous yes or no. Keep grain orientation distinct from rotation permission. Group parts by material, finish and thickness. Customer supplied boards require a workshop inspection policy.

FR 14 Should. Add an online table editor with add, duplicate, delete and material group actions. Validate positive finite dimensions, positive integer quantities, confirmed material options and machine limits. Set a configurable maximum row count, initially 200, and paginate or virtualise appropriately. Support manual review for unknown materials. Do not silently rotate grain sensitive panels or treat an incomplete list as production ready.

Acceptance: Launch requests accept either a valid attachment or an explicit request for measurement or list preparation help. In the later editor, invalid rows are identified before submission; copied rows preserve edge and grain specifications; out of range parts are flagged. An uploaded spreadsheet is stored as a document at launch, not automatically executed or parsed.

## 8 4 Attachments and submission behaviour

FR 15 Must. Accept JPEG, PNG, WebP, PDF and XLSX, with up to five files and 10 MB per file as an initial configurable limit. Explain allowed files and limits before upload. Exclude executables, macro enabled workbooks, archives and arbitrary CAD files at launch. Provide upload progress, removal, scan pending state and retry. Staff request alternative file formats through a controlled process if needed.

FR 16 Must. A successful submission creates a persistent record and a unique reference. Show the reference and next step only after the server confirms persistence. An idempotency key prevents a retry or double tap from creating duplicate records. Form details and files must be associated atomically where practical; failed or abandoned upload sessions expire and are cleaned up.

Acknowledgement email is sent when email is available. A phone only customer sees an on screen acknowledgement and receives staff contact through the selected channel. Optional customer messaging automation is a later integration. Notification failures must not destroy a stored enquiry or cause the interface to report a false submission failure.

Acceptance: Simulated email outage leaves the enquiry accessible to staff, marks delivery pending and retries. A network retry with the same key returns the original reference. Suspicious files remain quarantined and cannot be downloaded by staff until cleared. If scanning is unavailable, uploads remain pending rather than becoming public.

# 9 Staff lead and quotation workflow

## 9 1 Enquiry state model

FR 17 Must. Use New, Contacted, Awaiting Information, Assessment Scheduled, Assessment Complete, Quote in Preparation, Quote Sent, Won, Lost and Closed. Workshop enquiries can move from Contacted directly to Quote in Preparation. Invalid or abusive entries can be marked Spam with a reason. Every change records actor, timestamp and optional explanatory note. Allow correction or reopening by authorised staff with an audit reason.

Won means a business approved commercial confirmation exists and its evidence is recorded. A button click or an email open alone must not mark a job won. Quote acceptance is handled through the owner's approved contract process at launch. Quotation expired is a quote state, not necessarily a lost enquiry.

## 9 2 Assignment and follow up

FR 18 Must. Route service families to designated teams, flag out of area enquiries for review, and provide an unassigned queue. Store one owner, priority, follow up date, source, associated project and internal notes. Mixed enquiries have one coordinator. Email alerts direct employees to an authenticated record and do not attach private customer plans by default.

Display new enquiries, overdue follow ups and outstanding scans. Apply reminders only during configured operating hours. Proposed internal targets are first contact within one business day and an agreed quote date after information or assessment is complete. Avoid promising a final construction quote within 24 hours without operational support.

## 9 3 Quotes

FR 19 Must. Allow authorised estimators to attach a reviewed quote PDF and record quote number, revision, amount, currency, tax treatment, issue date, expiry date and delivery status. Only an authorised approver can send a quote. Sent revisions are immutable; new versions supersede previous ones while retaining the original record. Protect customer quote links using sufficiently random, expiring tokens; allow revocation and avoid indexable public URLs.

Formal quotes must state scope, materials, labour, exclusions, assumptions, measurement responsibility, approvals responsibility, delivery or installation terms, payment milestones, variation process and warranty terms as applicable. Tax settings depend on jurisdiction and verified registration, not a hardcoded assumption. Quote values use decimal money fields and explicit currency.

FR 20 Should. Introduce quote line item generation, PDF templates, recorded electronic acceptance of a specific version, declined reasons and expiring reminders. Keep assessments, estimates, quotations, contracts and invoices distinct. Later payment receipts and reconciliation must be linked to the correct accepted revision and contract.

Acceptance: An estimator cannot send a quote without approval authority. A superseded quote remains in staff history and is labelled accordingly on any accessible customer view. Notification provider failure triggers retry without creating another revision or sending uncontrolled duplicates.

# 10 Administration and content management

FR 21 Must. Provide a protected administration area supporting federated staff access grants, service availability, service areas, pages, FAQs, project records, image galleries, workshop catalogue and enquiry management. Use structured forms rather than allowing arbitrary scripts or layout HTML. Offer draft, preview, published and archived states, slug validation, ordering and reversible content revisions.

FR 22 Must. Portfolio uploads require captions, alternative text, stage labels, category tags and publication permission status. Create responsive derivatives, preserve approved originals separately and remove location metadata from public imagery. Show a preview before publication. No image is published without clearance.

FR 23 Must. Roles are Administrator, Sales Coordinator, Estimator, Content Editor and Read Only Reviewer. Administrator manages staff access grants and configuration without creating local passwords. Sales manages assigned enquiries. Estimator accesses authorised service enquiries and drafts quotes. Content Editor edits public content without customer file access. Reviewer sees only granted records. Owner approval permissions can be granted separately from the Administrator role.

FR 24 Must. Enforce permissions on the server for every record and file request. Provide audit history for content publication, user permission changes, enquiry export, quote issue and sensitive record access. Use deactivation instead of deleting users referenced in history. CSV exports are restricted, audited and protected against spreadsheet formula injection.

Acceptance: A content editor cannot retrieve customer attachments using a copied URL or API request. An archived service is absent from new public selection but remains on historic enquiries. Draft content is excluded from public APIs, sitemap and search indexing.

# 11 Visual design and content assets

Use a restrained, contemporary design reflecting the owner's actual work: neutral surfaces, a warm material accent, readable type, strong photographs and generous spacing. Final colours and logo depend on branding approval. Quality should come from authentic workmanship and clear information, with subtle movement only when useful.

The design system must define type sizes, spacing, colours, buttons, cards, labels, inputs, status chips, tables, dialogs, focus indicators, loading states and error states. Use one consistent service taxonomy across navigation, forms, projects and administration. Avoid separate visual identities for every service family.

FR 25 Must. Design and verify phone, tablet and desktop layouts, including narrow 320 CSS pixel screens. Ensure text wraps, navigation collapses sensibly and photographs retain appropriate crops. Provide reduced motion behaviour. Photo grids must reserve image space so loading does not move nearby controls.

Content inventory should include all three property profiles, available before and after photography, at least one detailed transformation case study, available examples across cabinetry categories, team and workshop imagery, the service list, delivery and material policies, FAQs, contact details and approved legal policies. Aim for a curated set of 30 to 60 photographs where available rather than an unedited image dump; launch with fewer strong images if necessary.

Record the photographer or licence, permission owner, allowed use, image stage and any privacy editing required. Do not invent testimonials to fill a layout. Owner developments are useful proof of delivery, but they do not establish experience serving external customers. Optional cost or income figures require evidence, dated context, publication approval and explanation of what is included.

## 12 2 Proposed API surface

Public endpoints: GET /api/v1/services; GET /api/v1/projects with filters and pagination; GET /api/v1/projects/{slug}; GET /api/v1/catalogue; POST /api/v1/upload-sessions; POST /api/v1/enquiries. Upload sessions use short lived scoped permissions, size limits and expiry, and are finalised only after server checks. Public GET endpoints expose published fields only.

Authenticated staff endpoints: GET /api/v1/admin/enquiries; GET and PATCH /api/v1/admin/enquiries/{id}; POST /api/v1/admin/enquiries/{id}/activities; POST /api/v1/admin/enquiries/{id}/assessments; POST /api/v1/admin/enquiries/{id}/quote-revisions; POST /api/v1/admin/quote-revisions/{id}/send; and controlled content, user, file and export endpoints.

Document schemas in OpenAPI, including enums, field limits, validation errors, pagination, authorisation and examples with synthetic data. Use consistent HTTP semantics: 201 on persisted creation, 400 or 422 for validation, 401 or 403 for access failures, 413 for excess size and 429 for rate limiting. Return a correlation ID with safe errors. Apply optimistic concurrency when staff edit the same record; warn rather than silently overwriting another user's update.

# 13 Security privacy and professional boundaries

## 13 1 Application security

NFR 01 Must. Use HTTPS, secure headers, appropriate CSP, secure cookies where applicable, server side validation, parameterised database access, sanitised rich text, explicit CORS policy and CSRF protection when cookie authenticated actions require it. Keep provider keys and database credentials server side. Enforce MFA for staff and promptly revoke departing users. Do not put private customer details into exception traces or page URLs.

NFR 02 Must. Apply rate limits, honeypot protection and an accessible anti bot challenge if abuse warrants it. Enforce upload restrictions using extension, MIME and signature checks where applicable, generated storage names, quarantine, malware scanning and authorisation. OWASP recommends layered validation and isolated storage because a filename or browser reported MIME type alone is insufficient. [S3]

Issue short lived signed download URLs only after authorised access checks, or proxy downloads when immediate revocation is required. Do not render unknown PDFs or spreadsheets in a public document viewer. Prevent public storage listing and log redacted security events. Test horizontal and role based access controls directly at API level.

## 13 2 Privacy operations

NFR 03 Must. Publish an accurate privacy notice identifying the business, purposes, data collected, processors, retention approach, contact and customer rights process. Collect only needed information; delay detailed plans and exact addresses when possible. In a South African deployment, assess POPIA obligations, operator agreements and any cross border processing with the actual hosting and messaging providers. POPIA addresses personal information processing and cross border flows. [S4]

Separate requested service communication from optional future marketing. Implement opt out and consent evidence for marketing if added, and have the owner approve the applicable basis and wording. The Information Regulator provides direct marketing guidance covering sections 11 and 69. [S5]

Proposed retention starting points are 12 months after closure for unsuccessful enquiries, 30 days for abandoned uploads and spam unless needed for an incident, and contract records retained under an approved accounting and legal policy. These are configurable business proposals, not statutory periods. Define deletion, correction, export, legal hold and backup expiry procedures before production. Select and approve a policy for nonessential analytics or third party embeds; forms must continue working when visitors decline optional tracking.

## 13 3 Building and design claims

The owner's style and conceptual ideas can be showcased, but the website must accurately identify who provides regulated professional services. SACAP's public guidance states that unregistered persons cannot provide architectural services in South Africa and recommends verifying registration and competence. Confirm the owner's status and any partner scope before advertising architectural design, plans submission or professional titles. [S6]

For property conversions and additions, the assessment process must identify which municipality and professional reviews are needed. The South African building standards framework concerns buildings within local authority jurisdiction; the actual project's zoning, development rights and approval requirements require location specific confirmation. [S7] Do not promise that every large plot can support additional rentable units.

The website should state responsibility for checking land use, approved plans, structural alterations, occupancy, fire and life safety, utilities, access and any rental or short stay constraints. These are project assessment topics, not determinations made by the website. Verify any builder registration, insurance, trade certification or supplier partnership before displaying badges. The software must not publish an assurance of legal compliance merely because a user completes an enquiry form.

# 14 Performance accessibility and search requirements

NFR 04 Must. Target WCAG 2.2 Level AA across public and staff journeys. Provide semantic headings, labelled controls, keyboard operated menus and galleries, visible unobscured focus, meaningful alt text, error summaries, sufficient contrast, usable target sizes and screen reader status announcements. Meet minimum target size requirements with applicable exceptions; aim for 44 by 44 CSS pixel primary touch controls. Automated checking alone does not establish conformance. [S1]

NFR 05 Must. Target good Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile of field visits when sufficient data exists: LCP no more than 2.5 seconds, INP no more than 200 milliseconds and CLS no more than 0.1. These are the published good thresholds. [S2] Before traffic exists, use repeatable mobile lab testing and interaction checks as proxies, then review field data.

Use responsive optimised images with appropriate browser fallbacks, reserved image dimensions, lazy loading below the fold and appropriate priority for the hero image. Set a proposed initial page transfer budget of 1.5 MB for key marketing pages and about 250 KB compressed JavaScript where achievable; validate against the selected implementation and actual imagery. These budgets are project targets, not industry guarantees. Avoid loading entire galleries, video libraries or large third party embeds on first visit.

NFR 06 Must. Provide crawlable rendered content, unique titles and descriptions, canonical URLs, XML sitemap, robots rules and structured data reflecting verified facts. Use appropriate business, service and breadcrumb data; do not fabricate review ratings. Apply noindex and access protection to admin, drafts and token based quote pages. Search directives are not a substitute for authentication. Handle redirects when slugs change and provide useful 404 pages.

Build service area content around places actually served, with verified contact consistency and genuine local project evidence. Add social sharing images and monitor search indexing. Use internal links among services, related case studies and enquiries. Do not rely on search ranking promises as an acceptance criterion.

# 15 Reliability and operations

NFR 07 Must. Proposed launch availability target is 99.5 percent per calendar month for public pages and enquiry submission, measured by synthetic checks and provider incident records. Confirm provider support and operating budget before adopting it contractually. Alert the designated operator on submission failure, persistent queue backlog, scan outage, email failure or storage problems.

NFR 08 Must. Back up database and private files with encryption, restricted access and retention. Proposed recovery objectives are no more than 24 hours data loss and restoration within eight business hours for a major failure. Use more frequent database recovery if the business needs it. A database backup without the corresponding attachment storage is incomplete. Perform a staging restore before launch and at least quarterly thereafter, recording results.

Monitor API latency, failed submissions, queue age, attachment scans, storage growth, email bounces and errors. Proposed API target is p95 under one second for metadata and lead persistence, excluding file transfer, scan and third party delivery, using an agreed staging workload initially 20 concurrent users and representative records. Benchmark and adjust before committing to contractual targets.

Provide support ownership, escalation contacts, dependency update policy, rollback steps, backup instructions, data export procedures, staff onboarding and offboarding, and an account ownership register. Use synthetic data in staging and prevent staging pages being indexed.

## 18 2 Suggestions with high practical value

Develop signature finish packages that suit compact accommodation, such as durable rental interiors and a more expressive short stay finish. Show material choices and maintenance advantages, with prices only after costing. Offer cabinetry bundles for repeat unit layouts when manufacturing capacity supports them.

Create one persuasive case study for each of the three properties rather than relying on a large gallery without context. Photograph the process, storage solutions, kitchens, exterior finishes and workshop production. Short, captioned project walk through videos can be added after core performance is established. A professionally prepared capability brochure can reuse approved website content later.

Build referral relationships with property managers, estate agents, registered architectural professionals, engineers and trade customers where appropriate. Give each referral source a measurable landing link; do not publish partnership logos without permission. A trade customer enquiry route can prioritise repeat cutting lists without requiring a wholesale platform.

Consider a paid feasibility assessment product with a defined written output: site observations, constraints, concept options, indicative scope and next steps, produced with appropriate professional input. The assessment must not be marketed as approved construction plans or a guaranteed investment result.

An optional later financial scenario tool can compare renovation costs and possible rental income. It should show user entered assumptions: number of units multiplied by monthly rent and occupancy, less operating costs, vacancy, management, maintenance, insurance and other relevant expenses. Show financing separately and define whether yield uses incremental development cost or total invested capital. Do not claim a payback result without specifying the denominator, excluded costs and cash flow assumptions. Keep professional review and dated market inputs outside the automated calculation.

Avoid an AI chatbot at launch unless there is a clear support need. Useful later automation would answer approved FAQs and gather enquiry context, hand off uncertain questions and avoid inventing prices, buildability, availability or approvals. A clear contact process is the immediate priority.

FR 26 Must. Use Angular presentation, Java Spring Boot business services and PostgreSQL persistence in a three-tier architecture. No customer sign-up, passwords, local staff password database or public staff registration is required. Maintain a minimal staff registry keyed by issuer and subject, with active state, role grants, teams and record scopes. Replace user account CRUD with staff access management. Enforce MFA through verified provider sign-in paths and use a local revocable staff session to enforce inactivity and absolute timeout.

Acceptance: A newly federated identity has no business access unless explicitly provisioned. Tokens with the wrong issuer, client or audience binding, expiry, signature or type are denied. Disabling a staff identity or session denies the next private request. Content editors cannot access private plans. Customers retain anonymous enquiry and scoped quote-link access.

FR 28 Must for email and integration contracts. Demo integrations use a test sink and generate no real messages or charges.

FR 29 Could for live payments. Provide a later hosted-gateway payment design, provisionally Payfast for an approved South African merchant. It links payments to accepted quote revisions and contract milestones, verifies provider notifications, deduplicates events and supports receipts, refunds and settlement reconciliation. Gateway selection and live activation remain pending. Payment-enabled flags are false at R1 launch; a quote request neither buys materials nor reserves stock.

Acceptance: Sandbox callback replay cannot duplicate a receipt; browser redirect alone cannot mark work paid; wrong merchant, amount or currency causes review; provider failure does not erase enquiry or quote records. These are later-release acceptance criteria, not a mandate to build a launch shop.
